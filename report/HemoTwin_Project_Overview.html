HemoTwin / V201 · The chair-side gap
01 · The chair-side gap

The dangerous minutes are the ones nobody is looking at.

During dialysis, fluid is removed faster than the body can refill the bloodstream. When plasma refill lags ultrafiltration, pressure falls — sometimes before anyone at the chair has a shared plan.

Haemodialysis filters blood outside the body. Blood leaves through vascular access, passes through a dialyzer, and returns. Ultrafiltration (UF) removes the fluid gained between sessions. Plasma refill moves fluid from tissue back into the vessels. If UF outruns refill, intravascular volume falls; the baroreflex compensates, then exhausts. The result is intradialytic hypotension (IDH) — here defined as a systolic pressure below 90 mmHg — often with symptoms, sometimes with a crash.

Illustrative session · fluid removal and pressurenot patient data

Who is at the chair?

Often a dialysis technician or nurse, while the nephrologist is remote. The next action — reduce UF, pause, give fluid, call for advice — happens before the doctor arrives. The missing piece is not another score. It is time and a shared trade-off.

The model can rank risk. It cannot make the response happen. That is why the product is a workflow layer, not a prediction dashboard.

Cuff cadence is often around 30 minutes. The warning window is 15–60 minutes. The session can change between two readings — which is why a projection matters.

02 · The product

One console. Four views. One shared state.

A device-agnostic session-state mirror. It does not control the machine. It makes the next review legible: where pressure is heading, what the unit's protocol says, and what each fluid option leaves behind.

HemoTwin / V205 · The decisive negative
05 · The decisive negative

We tested the twin. It did not work.

If reducing UF early raises SBP, the twin has a treatment effect. We froze the protocol before estimation and tested it on 581 matched events in 158 patients.

Design

  • Non-reactive UF step-down
  • Matched within-patient controls
  • Continuous dose-response
  • Pre-anchor rule
  • Sham-time negative control
  • 581 matched events / 158 patients
  • Fold 2 untouched

VERDICT · FAIL

The effect is indistinguishable from zero. The pre-registered sham-time control fails. P6a gets worse out of sample. The operational gain stays 0.

Why this page exists. A product that claims a treatment effect it cannot identify is not decision support. The negative result is the reason the rest of the console can be trusted.

HemoTwin / V207 · Why it matters
07 · Why it matters

It does not replace the nurse. It gives the call a shared state.

The product's real difference is not a better number on a dashboard. It is a shared, inspectable session state that lets the chair-side team act earlier and ask for help with the trade-off already on the table.

The workflow differenceillustrative

Earlier attention

Makes the next 60 minutes visible while the patient is still stable enough to review — not only after a cuff is already low.

Explicit trade-off

Each option carries a fluid cost and a next-session UF intensity. The conversation stops living in someone's head.

Shared handoff

Protocol step, role, FHIR bundle, bilingual shift card, local audit chain. The remote doctor sees the same state.

What “real difference” means here

The difference is not that the model is always right. The forecast is a calibrated conditional 60-minute estimate; the 90% band is marginal, not a guarantee. The difference is that the chair-side team can see the projection, the protocol step, and the fluid consequence together, and hand that state to a remote clinician without reconstructing it from memory.

Not yet proven. Workflow benefit has not been prospectively validated. The next experiment is a silent-mode pilot, then a pre-registered workflow evaluation — not another model. The product must earn that claim.

HemoTwin / V208 · The boundary
08 · The boundary

The boundary is part of the product.

The console says what it can and cannot do. That is not a disclaimer at the end; it is the design constraint that keeps the rest honest.

WE CAN CLAIM
  • A calibrated pre-anchor nadir forecast: fold-2 MAE 10.99, RMSE 14.65.
  • A 90% nadir band with marginal coverage: 89.2% HOLD / 90.1% TAPER.
  • Exact arithmetic fluid carry-forward for user-selected scenarios.
  • A deterministic, versioned review pathway with role-scoped steps.
  • A local FHIR R4 export, bilingual handoff card, and SHA-256 demo audit chain.
  • Honest negative results from the twin, event model, and external transport tests.
WE REFUSE TO CLAIM
  • A validated counterfactual or treatment effect from the twin.
  • That any UF policy will raise SBP, prevent IDH, or is minimum effective.
  • Alert utility, clinical benefit, or a safety layer.
  • Prospective, external, symptomatic-IDH, or Indian-unit validation.
  • A prescription, a default dose, or autonomous machine action.
  • Authentication, role enforcement, immutable audit, or machine integration.

Next evidence we owe

1 · Silent mode2–4 weeks beside the team. No machine control, no alert authority. Log what the console would have shown.
2 · Local labelsHarmonise the IDH definition, symptoms, and nursing actions; recalibrate on partner-machine data.
3 · Workflow trialPre-registered prospective evaluation of time-to-review, escalation, and fluid carry-forward.
4 · IndiaNo Indian cohort yet. The method travels; the weights do not. Retraining and local recalibration are prerequisites.

Why the boundary earns trust

A system that knows what it cannot do is easier to audit, easier to pilot, and harder to misuse. The G7 failure is not hidden in an appendix: it is on the evidence page and in the product copy. The twin is an illustration; the forecast and the arithmetic are the validated surfaces.

Deployment context. India has roughly 13,800 dialysis machines across 751 PMNDP districts and 1–1.5 nephrologists per million people. The cohort is 34.5% diabetic. Those numbers describe the need, not an efficacy claim.

What would change our mind

Local recalibration changing the forecast, or a workflow trial showing no time benefit.

What would stop the pilot

An alert burden that outweighs the value, or a trade-off that cannot be used at the chair.

What we will report

The same numbers either way: time-to-review, escalation, fluid carry-forward, and nurse usability.

HemoTwin / V209 · Judge Q&A
09 · Judge Q&A

The questions we expect — answered plainly.

Answer, then stop talking. The evidence page has the numbers; this page has the short form.

What is HemoTwin?
One console with four views: Case lab, Live session, Session replay, and Evidence & handoff. It mirrors the session, structures the unit's review pathway, and makes the fluid arithmetic explicit. It does not prescribe.
Why not just predict IDH?
Because ranking is not timing. Our event model ranked at AUROC 0.880 but detected only 8.2% of event sessions at the pre-registered alert budget — below the current-SBP rule at 20.2%. The product is the workflow layer; the model stays shadow.
Did the physiological twin work?
No. G3 is identical at gain 0. G7 gives +0.13 mmHg per 0.25 L/h with a 95% CI of [−2.63, +3.06]; the sham control fails; P6a gets worse. Verdict FAIL. The fan is a labelled illustration and is off by default.
Which forecast is which?
The console uses the fair 59-feature pre-anchor model: MAE 10.99, no future-UF oracle. The 9.60 is an oracle-assisted display forecast with 13 observed future-UF features. It is not the G2 baseline and not a better twin.
Is this a medical device?
No. It is a research workflow prototype. It does not diagnose, prescribe, control the machine, or claim clinical benefit. The role selector is a recorded label, not enforcement.
Is the audit trail real?
It is a local SHA-256 chain over demo events. It is append-only by construction, but not authenticated, not tamper-evident, and not an immutable clinical record. The app says so.
You have no Indian data.
Correct. HEMOBP is Taiwanese; the Spanish transport check failed at AUROC 0.474 and the labels differ. No Indian cohort has been collected. The method travels; the weights do not. Local retraining and recalibration are prerequisites.
What is the evidence?
205,482 QC-eligible HEMOBP sessions, 1,070 patients, 4,190,926 telemetry rows, CC BY 4.0. G7 uses 205,113 sessions; 369 lack the required pre-anchor record. Labelled hypotension is 10.4% (nadir <90); the Spanish symptomatic label is 25.5% — different labels, never combined. Fold 2 was untouched.
What would a pilot look like?
A silent-mode run beside the team for 2–4 weeks: no machine control, no alert authority, log what the console would have shown. Then local recalibration, then a pre-registered prospective evaluation of time-to-review and workflow. The next experiment is a workflow, not another model.
What do you refuse to claim?
A validated counterfactual or treatment effect from the twin; that any UF policy will raise SBP, prevent IDH, or is minimum effective; alert utility, clinical benefit, or a safety layer; prospective, external, symptomatic-IDH, or Indian-unit validation; a prescription, default dose, or autonomous machine action.

Three-minute demo path

  1. Case lab — hover a cuff for calibrated + observed SBP; click for SBP, DBP, UF and the twin state.
  2. Fluid arithmetic — the projection is shared; the table shows what changes.
  3. Evidence — open G7, the claim boundary, the FHIR bundle, the audit chain.
  4. Replay / Live — watch time move; enter a session and evaluate.
