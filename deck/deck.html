The problem

A dialysis session is a four-hour tightrope

Remove too little fluid and the patient stays overloaded. Remove it too fast and the pressure falls. A systolic below 90 mmHg during the session is intradialytic hypotension — IDH.

2× vascular-access thrombosis in the highest IDH quartileChang, JASN 2011
9.4× intradialytic arrhythmia with IDHPun, CJASN 2021
1.57 hazard ratio for mortality when nadir systolic falls below 90Flythe, NDT 2018 · association, not proof of causation

The event takes minutes.
The measurement takes half an hour.

Why India

1,700+ district centres. No nephrologist on site.

“This is the terminal ward of India’s T2D epidemic.”

34.5% of our dialysis cohort is diabetic (370 of 1,072 patients). India has about 101.3 million people with diabetes and 136 million with prediabetes (ICMR-INDIAB 2023). Kidney failure is where that epidemic ends — on a machine, three times a week.

One nephrologist cannot be in 1,700 buildings.
Remote supervision is not a feature. It is the only arithmetic that works.

1,700+ dialysis centres under the Pradhan Mantri National Dialysis Programme, many in district hospitals without on-site nephrology → remote supervision is the only scalable model
156 sessions per patient per year — three a week, for a median dialysis vintage of 9.6 years → no home sensor produces this density
~34M dialysis sessions a year in India → an estimate, labelled as one

Layer 01

Event model

40 features, patient-disjoint split. Answers one question: will systolic fall below 90 in the next 15–60 minutes?

Layer 02

Physiological twin

Holds a volume state for this patient, with two named parameters. Predicts the next BP, then corrects when the cuff fires.

V(t) · krefill · βbp

Layer 03

Counterfactual engine

Projects pressure over the next hour under hold, taper and stop, and does the fluid accounting to session end. Returns a nadir with an interval, and the fluid each policy leaves behind.

3 policies · empirical band · litres left
Action sheet Taper to X mL/h · recheck in 15 minutes · escalate to the remote nephrologist if nadir < 90 mmHg. Nothing fires automatically.

What the headline does not say

86.0% of crash sessions with a usable warning window (1,783 / 2,073). 281 sessions had no anchor in the window — a cuffing-cadence limit, reported not hidden.

1,268,990 prediction anchors, 32,221 positives (2.54%), 1,070 patients, split by patient. And our 15–60 min AUROC is not comparable to a 15–75 min result.

Our AUPRC of 0.236 is higher than the best published external AUPRC (Kim 2022, 0.106), on a different cohort and a narrower horizon. We do not treat that as a like-for-like win. We lead with detection and alarm burden, not the area under the curve.

The twin

Two equations a nephrologist can argue with

State · volume
dV/dt = −UF(t)+ krefill · (Vtarget − V(t))

Ultrafiltration pulls volume out. Tissue refills plasma at a rate set by krefill. The deeper the deficit, the harder the refill pushes back. That is the whole volume model.

krefill — per hour · starts from a population prior, updated from the patient’s own sessions
Output · pressure
SBP(t) = SBP0 + βbp · (V(t) − V0)− γ · elapsed(t)

Systolic pressure rides on the volume still in the patient, scaled by βbp, minus a slow drift with time on the machine.

βbp — mmHg per litre · the number that differs most between patients
PredictForward to the next cuff
→
MeasureThe next real BP arrives
→
CorrectState and both parameters update

Two personal parameters, not twenty. With a cuff every ~30 minutes, anything richer is not identifiable from one session — it is curve-fitting with a nicer diagram. We chose the model a nephrologist can audit, then checked its volume state against measured weight loss.

Two things this buys

0.0410 is not a knife-edge. It sits on the flat of the curve — the net-benefit optimum is 0.0500, worth +0.0442. A threshold frozen on validation lands on the same plateau as the one chosen on test.

We beat "alarm on everyone" at every cost ratio tested (pt = 0.05, 0.10, 0.20), not only at one operating point. A partner unit can retune the threshold to its own staffing cost without touching the model.

Trust

Why a nurse should believe the number

01 · Calibration

Probabilities, not scores

A reliability diagram, a Brier score, and the cohort base rate printed beside every risk. A 50% that means 50 out of 100.

02 · Alarm budget

The nursing team picks the trade

Threshold fixed on validation at 0.0410. On test: sensitivity 0.862, 20.6% false-alarm session rate. The operating point is a staffing decision, and it is shown, not buried.

03 · Drivers

SHAP, translated

“UF rate above her usual.” “One hundred and ten minutes into the session.” “Refill index below her own baseline.” Reasons a nurse can check.

04 · Audit

Every override is data

Recommendation, override, recheck and outcome are timestamped and exportable. Overrides are reviewed weekly — they mark where the model and the ward disagree.

05 · Subgroups

Pre-specified, not yet run

Vintage, diabetes, sex, age band. The analysis is specified in the model card and will be published with intervals — including where it is worse. We do not claim fairness we have not measured.

Limitations

What this cannot do yet

One cohort; the external check failed

Taiwanese data only, internally validated. A session model scored 0.720 internally and 0.474 in Spain, because the labels mean different things. No Indian cohort. Deployment means retraining, never transplanting probabilities.

Failure reported in docs/EXTERNAL_VALIDATION.md

A cuff every ~30 minutes

281 crash sessions had no anchor inside a warnable window. The model cannot see what the machine does not measure.

Reported as 75.7% of all, 86.0% of warnable

“SBP under 90” is a definition

Not a symptom. We predict a number crossing a threshold, not dizziness, cramps or a patient who feels awful.

Symptom labels are not in these cohorts

The counterfactual is observational

Within-policy-space simulation: everyone tapers, so we are choosing timing, not inventing an intervention. Validated on held-out patients, with intervals.

A decision aid, not a prescription

Not a medical device

No regulatory clearance. No diagnosis, no prescription, no automatic action. It is a research prototype in front of clinicians.

CDSCO pathway not started

Alarm burden is real

20.6% of uncomplicated sessions raise an alarm. That is a ward cost, and the reason the threshold is a nursing decision.

Tunable; shown, not hidden
